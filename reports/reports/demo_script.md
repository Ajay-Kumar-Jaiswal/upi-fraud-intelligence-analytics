# Demo Script (3–5 minutes)
All numbers below are pulled directly from `reports/analytics_validation.md` and
`reports/DATA_QUALITY_EVIDENCE.md` (regenerated by the pipeline) — nothing here is invented.

## 0:00–0:30 — Business problem
"A National Payments Authority needs to find fraud rings, synthetic identities, and
compromised merchants in a UPI transaction dataset that's intentionally messy. We built a
full data-rescue-to-dashboard pipeline — and the most important thing we found wasn't a
fraud pattern, it was a **data-integrity problem** the authority needs to know about before
trusting any identity-linked number."

## 0:30–1:00 — Messy data challenge
"Four files, ~66,000 raw rows. Currency symbols in amount fields, 6 different ID formats
per file, Unix epoch timestamps mixed with 5 other date formats, 14 spellings of
'success/failed,' 82 raw merchant category labels." *(show `reports/DATA_AUDIT.md` scroll)*

## 1:00–2:00 — Data rescue
"We cleaned all four files down to a validated model with 97–99.8% row retention — we only
ever drop exact duplicates, everything else gets flagged and kept." *(show
`reports/DATA_QUALITY_EVIDENCE.md` cleaning table)* "And here's the finding that shapes
everything downstream: `user_id` and `merchant_id` joins from transactions to the KYC and
merchant-master files match at only 32% and 48%. We didn't just eyeball that — we tested it
against what pure random ID overlap would produce given the ID ranges, and it's
statistically indistinguishable from chance. So instead of pretending we have a working
identity join, every chart that depends on it shows its own coverage percentage."
*(Dashboard → Data Quality & Coverage page)*

## 2:00–2:45 — Key analytics
*(Dashboard → Executive Overview)* "20,000 clean transactions, ₹250 million, 85% success
rate. 2,800 chargebacks, a 13% chargeback-to-transaction ratio — computed via the ONE
identity join that actually works in this data, chargeback-to-transaction, at 93%
coverage." *(Dashboard → Merchant Intelligence, scroll to bonus question)* "The official
bonus question — highest chargeback ratio by merchant category this quarter — Travel, at
15.67%. We also show you it's a thin margin over Retail at 15.57%, on a modest sample. We'd
rather tell you that than oversell a coin-flip as a confident finding."

## 2:45–3:30 — Fraud/risk engine, merchant intelligence, network
*(Dashboard → Fraud & Risk)* "Every risk score is rule-based and explainable — click any
flagged merchant and you get the exact reasons, like 'chargeback ratio above 30%,' not a
black-box number. 3 merchants are CRITICAL, 38 HIGH-or-above out of 658 with enough volume."
*(Dashboard → Network Analysis)* "And here's an honest negative finding: in this data
window, no user ever transacts with the same merchant twice, and no two merchants share
even two common users. The transaction graph is genuinely sparse — we say so, instead of
manufacturing a 'ring' the data doesn't support."

## 3:30–4:00 — AI Analyst
*(Dashboard → AI Analyst, type a question live)* "Ask it anything in our supported set —
'Which merchant category has the highest chargeback ratio this quarter' — and it calls the
exact same tested function powering the dashboard page you just saw. No hallucination risk,
because it's not generating the number, it's calling code that already has 121 passing
tests behind it."

## 4:00–4:30 — Business impact & closing
"Bottom line for the authority: don't trust identity-linked fraud claims from this dataset
at face value — the join simply isn't reliable enough yet, and we've quantified exactly how
unreliable. What IS reliable: transaction-level KPIs, merchant-category risk, and the
chargeback-to-transaction link. That's a more useful, more honest starting point than a
dashboard that quietly assumes joins work when they don't."

## Strongest 3–5 insights for judges
1. **The identity-join limitation itself** — tested against a random-chance baseline, not
   just observed, and propagated as a visible caveat into every affected metric.
2. **Chargeback-to-transaction ratio: 13.04%**, computed via the one join in this dataset
   confirmed to be reliable (93.1% coverage).
3. **Bonus question answered dynamically** with its margin and coverage limitation stated
   up front (Travel 15.67% vs Retail 15.57%).
4. **Explainable risk engine**: 3 CRITICAL / 38 HIGH-or-above merchants, each with named,
   auditable reasons — no black box.
5. **Honest negative network finding**: maximally sparse transaction graph (no repeated
   user–merchant pairs, no shared-user merchant clusters) — reported as-is rather than
   manufactured into a fraud-ring narrative.
